Is it perfect now?
Not fully.

Because the detection is based on speech recognition text, not direct audio understanding.

That means:

if browser speech recognition is slow, the mute may be late
if speech recognition misses the word, nothing happens
accuracy depends on browser support and mic clarity
Can it be improved?
Yes.

Best possible improvements:

Better buffering logic

keep a slightly smarter rolling buffer
drop or replace the exact chunk where profanity is detected
this would make censorship cleaner
Faster detection

current method waits for browser speech recognition
a faster or more reliable speech engine would improve it
Word-level timing

detect when in the audio the bad word happened
mute only that exact section instead of a rough short mute
Server or AI-assisted moderation

more accurate than browser-only recognition
but adds cost, delay, and privacy concerns
Hybrid moderation

browser does instant first-pass filtering
backend does smarter verification/logging if needed
Practical answer
Yes, it can definitely be improved.

Your current system is a good lightweight version:

simple
real-time
browser-based
privacy-friendly
But it is not the most accurate or most advanced version yet.

One-line summary
Right now the app says:
“Hold audio briefly, turn speech into text, and if a bad word appears, mute that section before sending the processed audio.”
